What business process automation is

Business process automation means using software to complete routine steps that people currently do by hand. Traditional automation is good at fixed rules. AI adds the ability to read emails and documents, so more of the work can be automated while people keep the final say on important decisions.

Processes we automate

  • Email: sort incoming mail, draft replies and send routine answers. See our add-on plan: $349 setup, then $39 a month.
  • Documents: pull key details from invoices, forms and PDFs.
  • Data entry: move information between systems without retyping.
  • Reporting: build regular reports automatically.
  • Approvals: send requests to the right person and remind them.
  • Customer updates: send booking, order and status messages.

How to choose what to automate first

Good first candidates share four things: the task happens often, it follows clear steps, mistakes are easy to catch, and it takes up real staff time. Start with one process, prove it works, then move to the next. Our guide lists 10 tasks most businesses can automate.

Keeping people in control

Automation should not be a black box. We keep a person in the loop for sensitive or high-value decisions, and the system flags anything it is unsure about. Larger companies can read about our enterprise engagement models. Use the savings calculator for a rough estimate of the time involved.

An example: invoice processing

A supplier emails an invoice as a PDF. Someone normally opens it, types the details into the accounting tool and asks a manager to approve it. With automation, AI reads the supplier, date, amount and invoice number. The system fills in a draft, flags anything unusual and sends it for approval. A person still approves the payment, but the typing has gone.

How to measure the result

Write down three numbers before you begin: how often the task happens, how long it takes and how often mistakes occur. Check the same numbers after launch. This shows clearly whether the automation is earning its keep, and it helps you decide what to automate next. If a task is rare or changes constantly, the answer may be to leave it alone.

What we need to get started

  • A person who knows how the process works today
  • Examples of the emails, documents or reports involved
  • A list of the tools the process touches
  • Access to a safe way of testing
  • Someone to approve the finished workflow
