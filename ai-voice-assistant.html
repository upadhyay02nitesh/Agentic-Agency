AI Voice Assistants That Answer Your Business Calls

An AI voice assistant picks up when your team cannot. It answers common questions, books appointments and tells you what happened.

What an AI voice assistant does

When a customer calls and nobody answers, that enquiry may go to someone else. An AI voice assistant answers the call, talks naturally about everyday requests and takes the next step, such as booking an appointment or taking a message.

You choose when it answers. Some businesses use it only after hours or when the line is busy. Others use it as the first point of contact.

What it can handle

  • Answering questions about services, prices, hours and location
  • Booking, moving and cancelling appointments in your calendar
  • Taking messages and sending you a short summary
  • Putting urgent calls through to the right person
  • Sending a text confirmation after the call

Sensitive or unusual calls should go to a person. We agree those rules with you before launch.

How we set it up

  1. Map your calls. We list your most common calls and what should happen on each one.
  2. Write the rules. We agree what it may answer and when it must pass a call to your team.
  3. Connect your tools. We link your phone number and calendar.
  4. Test and launch. You test it with realistic calls before customers reach it.

You can usually keep your existing number by forwarding calls to the assistant.

Being open with callers

We recommend telling callers they are speaking with an AI assistant and always letting them ask for a person. Rules on AI calls and call recording differ by country, so we plan the setup around where your customers are. If you need legal certainty, check with your own adviser.

When a voice assistant makes sense

A voice assistant suits businesses where customers still prefer to call. Good signs include a phone that rings while staff are with customers, calls that come in after you close, and a team that repeats the same answers all day.

It is less suitable when almost every call is complicated or emotional. In that case, it may work better as an overflow option that takes a message and gets the right person to call back.

A typical call, step by step

  1. A customer calls your usual number, and the call forwards to the assistant when your team is busy.
  2. The assistant greets them with your business name and says it is an AI assistant.
  3. The customer asks for an appointment in their own words.
  4. The assistant checks your calendar and offers open times.
  5. The booking is saved and a text confirmation goes out.
  6. You receive a short summary of the call.

What to prepare

  • A list of your most common calls
  • Your calendar and booking rules
  • The people to contact for urgent calls
  • The greeting you want callers to hear
  • Your preferred languages

We test the assistant with difficult examples too, such as background noise, people changing their mind and questions it should refuse to answer.

Frequently asked questions

Will an AI voice assistant sound robotic?

Modern voice AI sounds natural in everyday conversations such as bookings and opening hours. You can hear and test it before it goes live.

Can it transfer a call to a real person?

Yes. We set rules for which calls go straight to you or your team, such as urgent requests.

Do I need a new phone number?

Usually not. Most setups forward your existing number when you are busy or closed.

Does it replace my receptionist?

It does not have to. Many businesses use it to cover busy periods and after-hours calls, so staff can focus on the customers in front of them.
