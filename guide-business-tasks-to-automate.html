10 Business Tasks You Can Automate With AI

Every business has jobs that repeat each week. Here are ten of the most practical tasks to automate, and how to choose where to start.

By NextGen AI Labs · Updated 31 October 2026

In this guide
  1. Customer-facing tasks
  2. Sales tasks
  3. Back-office tasks
  4. How to choose your first task
  5. What not to automate yet
  6. A quick example of the scoring method
  7. What each task needs
  8. Check before you switch it on

Customer-facing tasks

  1. Answering common questions with an AI chatbot.
  2. Answering phone calls with an AI voice assistant.
  3. Booking appointments directly into your calendar.
  4. Sending reminders to reduce no-shows.

Sales tasks

  1. Replying to new leads straight away.
  2. Following up with people who have not booked or bought yet.
  3. Requesting reviews after a visit or sale.

Back-office tasks

  1. Sorting email and drafting routine replies.
  2. Entering invoice data from PDFs into your accounting tool.
  3. Building regular reports from your existing data.

See more on business process automation.

How to choose your first task

Score each task from 1 to 5 on three questions: How often does it happen? How much time does it take? How simple are the steps? Start with the highest score, as long as a mistake would be easy to catch and fix.

What not to automate yet

Leave out tasks that need careful judgement or carry serious risk, such as medical advice, legal decisions or handling a very upset customer. AI can prepare the information, but a person should stay in charge.

A quick example of the scoring method

Suppose appointment reminders score 5 for frequency, 3 for time and 5 for simplicity, a total of 13. Monthly strategy reports might score 1, 4 and 1, a total of 6. Reminders are the clear first choice: they happen often, the steps are simple and a mistake is easy to spot. The score is only a guide, but it stops you from starting with the hardest job.

What each task needs

  • Customer questions: approved answers and a handover rule.
  • Bookings and reminders: access to your calendar.
  • Lead replies and follow-ups: a connected form or CRM, and approved wording.
  • Reviews: a trigger after each visit or sale, and your review link.
  • Email: rules for what can be sent and what must be checked first.
  • Invoices and reports: sample documents and access to your accounting or data tools.

Check before you switch it on

Run each automation on test data first. Check that it handles missing details, unusual requests and duplicates. Then let a person review its work for the first few days. Once you trust the results, you can reduce those checks, but keep an occasional review in place.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest task to automate first?

Answering common customer questions is often the easiest place to start, because the answers are already known and easy to check.

How long does it take to automate a task?

A simple task can take days, while connecting several systems can take weeks. A written plan should confirm the timing.
